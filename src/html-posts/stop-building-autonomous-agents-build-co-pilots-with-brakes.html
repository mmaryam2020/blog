Stop building autonomous agents. Build co-pilots with brakes.

The best agents aren’t the ones that need humans least. They’re the ones humans can steer.

#ai #agents #human-in-the-loop #ux #architecture

I keep watching the same hype.

Engineers ship a loop that “self-heals.” Demo looks magic. Production meets a messy API, an ambiguous rule, a domain edge nobody put in the prompt. The agent doesn’t quietly ask for help. It rationalizes. It retries. It burns tokens. By step ten, step two’s mistake is holy scripture.

Meanwhile the person who could have fixed it in two seconds (claims, underwriting, clinic, compliance) is waiting in an inbox labeled “review.”

That’s asynchronous human-in-the-loop. It feels like control. It’s mostly cleanup.

Click to zoom

Inside the loop, live

What actually helps isn’t removing the human. It’s putting them inside the loop, live.

1
Plan gate

Before the agent runs wild. Check the goal and the steps before it burns context or wanders off.

2
Critique gate

Before anything mutates a real system. A human approves the change in one click.

3
Branch-and-steer UI

Instead of “please rewrite the system prompt.” Steer it live with quick controls, instead of fixing the prompt after it fails.

A better cockpit

Chat boxes are a terrible cockpit for that work. Experts think in tables, diffs, checklists, decision trees.

Progressive agency beats fake full autonomy: suggest three options, then bounded steps with pause, then monitored paths with automatic escalation.

From step ten to step two

Go back to that person in the review inbox. The one who could have fixed it in two seconds.

Move them from step ten to step two. Give them a pause button, three options, and a way to fork the run. The agent still does the heavy lifting. They just get to steer before it matters.

Zoom out

More autonomy without brakes isn’t sophistication. It’s a runaway car.

Build the tandem bike instead: the model pedals, the human steers at every intersection.

And save every correction they make. That’s your next eval.
